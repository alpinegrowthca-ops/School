# DESIRE × construction crews: “The Rivet Toss” (Seedance 2.5, 28 s, 9:16, 2D cartoon)

## The idea in one line

In a black-and-white **1930s rubber-hose cartoon**, a skyscraper crew tosses hot coffee up the steel the way ironworkers once tossed red-hot rivets, and it's a disaster. Then the heater tosses up a **DESIRE tin**. It's the only thing in colour, and when it's caught, **colour floods the whole world**, the scratchy jazz flips into a modern beat, and the old newsreel narrator suddenly sounds crystal clear. **“Nicotine free. Hands free.”**

## Why this will stop thumbs and sell tins

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **1930s rubber-hose black-and-white** | The most distinctive frame in any Reels feed: grain, flicker and bendy limbs read instantly as “something different”. It's pure pattern interrupt, and nobody in the caffeine-pouch category looks like this ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/video-ad-hooks-drive-conversions), [TrendTrack](https://www.trendtrack.io/blog-post/best-performing-ad-hooks)). |
| **The rivet toss** | Real ironworker history. Crews heated rivets at a forge and tossed them up to a catcher with a bucket. Tradespeople know it and respect it. Swapping rivets for coffee cups is an instant, wordless gag, and it tells the product truth: coffee doesn't travel up a steel frame; a tin does (dossier §2.4, “DESIRE competes where coffee is impossible”). |
| **Black-and-white → colour when the tin is caught** | A visual *transformation* moment, which is the “before/after” hook in one frame. The product literally brings the world to life, with no health claim needed. The audio flip (crackly mono → clean modern, jazz → beat) makes a combined visual + audio hook, which Meta reports is 1.5× more likely to drive purchase intent ([Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182)). |
| **Newsreel narrator** | Carries the facts with charm, needs no lip-sync (fewer AI failures), and makes “Adults eighteen and over!” a joke instead of a disclaimer. |
| **Sell control, not energy** | Tradespeople read “energy” as weakness (dossier §1.4). The hero never gets a buzz. He just gets *organized*: tin in the pocket, both hands on the rivet gun. |
| **Verified claims only** | Nicotine free, 80mg, the same dose in all three flavours, some cans carry 300mg, three flavours, 18+ (dossier §2.3, §5.1, §10.C). No “fast release”, “no jitters” or “focus” (unproven or restricted), nothing about quitting (§9.5), no L-tyrosine (§8.3). |
| **Meta review-proof** | Meta bans nicotine-pouch ads ([policy](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/tobacco-related-products/)). The pouch never goes near a mouth, “caffeine” and “nicotine-free” are in the first caption and the first narrator product line, and there's no dip or snus slang. |
| **Campaign consistency** | It ends on the same line as “Coffee Can't Climb” (“Nicotine free. Hands free.”), so two very different creatives build one memorable brand line. |

## If something goes wrong

- **Colour leaks into phase 1, or phase 2 stays grey:** generate it as two clips, 0:00–0:12.5 (the black-and-white prompt blocks) and 0:12.5–0:28 (colour), and cut on the catch. The cut hides inside the colour burst.
- **Tin looks cartoonish or warped:** use the tin photos as references. If it's still off, generate shots 7, 9 and 10 with the tin photo as the first frame.
- **Ray or Hank change between the two phases:** make character sheets first (black-and-white and colour side by side), add them as references, and regenerate.
- **Narrator voice changes when the audio upgrades:** generate the narrator with a voice tool once, clean. Apply the vintage filter yourself in edit for 0–12.5 s and upload it as the audio reference.
- **Too long for your platform:** 28 s fits Seedance 2.5's 30 s window. If your plan caps shorter, use the two-clip split above.
