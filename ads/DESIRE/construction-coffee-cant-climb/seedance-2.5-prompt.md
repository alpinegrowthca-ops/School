# DESIRE × construction crews: “Coffee Can't Climb” (Seedance 2.5, 23 s, 9:16)

## The idea in one line

A panicking clay coffee cup can't climb 40 floors of steel to reach an ironworker, and a jittery energy can isn't much help either. The one *real* object in this clay world, the DESIRE tin, rides up in his vest pocket. **“Nicotine free. Hands free.”**

## Why this concept (research → decisions)

| Insight | Source | How the ad uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Don't sell tradespeople “energy”; energy implies weakness. Sell **control**: a measured dose you chose, not a 32 oz can the truck stop had | Dossier §1.4, hook bank #27–28 | The hero is a calm, unbothered veteran. The loud can is the comic foil |
| DESIRE doesn't compete with coffee on effect. **It competes in the situations where coffee is impossible** | Dossier §2.4; the Army built non-drink caffeine because soldiers couldn't stop to brew (§8.1) | 40 floors up, gloves on, clipped to a beam: a cup can't get there. The core gag *is* the product truth: no cup, no can, hands free |
| Some energy drinks carry 300 mg in one can | Dossier §2.3 [verified] | The can's only brag, played as overkill, not danger (the villain is never caffeine, §7.5) |
| Flavour comes up 45× more than energy in reviews | Dossier §3.1 | A dedicated flavour beat with tactile “pop” bursts |
| “Nicotine free” has to work hard; buyers assume every pouch has nicotine | Dossier §4.5 | The first caption opens with “Nicotine-free caffeine…”, it's printed on the tin, and it's in the end line |
| **Meta bans nicotine-pouch ads**, so a caffeine pouch that *looks* like one risks rejection | [Meta ad standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/tobacco-related-products/) | The pouch is **never shown going into a mouth**. No dip, lip or snus language. “Caffeine” and “Nicotine free” are on screen early. 18+ |
| Hook in about 2 s, built for sound-off with captions, and visual + audio hooks together do best (1.5×). 15–30 s for Reels | [Social Media Today on Meta's tips](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182), [Cropink](https://cropink.com/reels-ads), [Vidico](https://vidico.com/news/facebook-video-ad-best-practices-that-actually-work-2026/) | Frame 1 is a screaming cup dangling over a 40-storey drop: a visual oddity, a scream and an identity callout at once. Captions carry the story with sound off |
| Pattern interrupts win, and the Reels feed is mostly live-action phone footage | [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/video-ad-hooks-drive-conversions), [TrendTrack](https://www.trendtrack.io/blog-post/best-performing-ad-hooks) | Bright claymation stands out instantly. The photoreal tin inside the clay world is the visual metaphor: *the one real, measured thing up here* |
| Tradespeople roast bad safety in comments | Audience knowledge | Correct PPE throughout: harness clipped, tools tethered, nothing dropped from height (the can rockets **up**, not down) |

**Left out on purpose:** “fast release”, “no jitters” and “helps focus” (unproven, or blocked in some markets; see `../truck-gag/script.md`), anything about quitting nicotine (§9.5), L-tyrosine (§8.3), and real brands or logos. The can's twitchiness is comedy, not a comparative claim.

## The prompt (copy everything in the box)

```text
FORMAT
A 23-second vertical 9:16 multi-shot animated ad with native synchronized audio: character dialogue with lip-sync, one off-screen voice line, sound effects and music. Nine shots joined by hard cuts on the beat; no fades, no title cards, no text.

STYLE (identical in every shot)
Bright, saturated 3D claymation stop-motion look: hand-sculpted plasticine characters with visible fingerprint texture and soft rounded forms, miniature handmade sets, warm golden sunrise light, crisp soft-edged shadows, gentle tilt-shift depth of field, playful exaggerated cartoon physics, smooth motion with a subtle handmade stop-motion charm. The DESIRE tins are the ONLY photorealistic objects in the whole video: real matte-black metal with sharp, correctly spelled printed lettering, as if a real product were placed into the clay world. Colour palette: sunrise orange and pink sky, red-primer steel, safety-orange hi-vis, yellow crane, with the tins' mint-teal, watermelon-pink and berry-purple rims as the brightest accents.

REFERENCES
@image1 = Cool Mint tin, @image2 = Watermelon Ice tin, @image3 = Mixed Berry tin: match shape, finish, logo, lettering and rim colour exactly. @image4 (if supplied) = Dale's character sheet.

CONTINUITY LOCK (never redesign anything between shots)
DALE: a burly adult ironworker in his mid-forties made of clay, with warm tan skin, a big square jaw, a thick salt-and-pepper beard, bushy eyebrows, small calm eyes, a slightly crooked nose and a barrel chest. He wears a scuffed white hard hat with a few faded stickers, clear safety glasses, a fluorescent-orange hi-vis vest with silver reflective stripes and a zippered chest pocket over a charcoal long-sleeve work shirt, tan canvas work pants, a yellow full-body safety harness clipped to the steel by a short lanyard at all times, tan leather work gloves, and brown steel-toe boots.
CUPPA: a small clay paper-coffee-cup character, about knee-high to Dale, with a white cup body, a brown corrugated sleeve, a white plastic sip lid, tiny stick arms and legs, huge round panicked eyes and a wobbly open mouth. Faint wisps of steam rise from its lid until shot 8.
BIG CAN: a hulking clay energy-drink-can character, twice Cuppa's height, with a dented brushed-silver body, a jagged neon-yellow lightning stripe with no logo or text, a pull tab bent up like a crooked cap, bulging bloodshot eyes, a twitchy manic grin, veiny little arms, and a constant nervous vibration.
TINS (photoreal): each a slim round matte-black metal tin about the width of a palm and two centimetres tall, with a flush pressed lid, the word DESIRE in clean white uppercase sans-serif lettering centred on the lid, a small white NICOTINE FREE line beneath the logo, and a thin flavour ring around the lid edge: mint-teal (Cool Mint), watermelon-pink (Watermelon Ice), deep berry-purple (Mixed Berry). When open, each holds a neat ring of small white rounded-corner pouches. Rim colours never swap or blend.
SET: a skyscraper under construction 40 storeys up: exposed red-primer steel I-beams and columns, orange safety netting along the edges, a yellow tower crane behind, and a miniature clay city far below glowing in warm sunrise light under a pink-orange sky.

VOICES (keep each identical every time it returns)
DALE: deep, gravelly adult male voice in his mid-forties, neutral North American accent, slow unbothered pace, dry deadpan delivery, understated confidence, like a veteran foreman who has seen everything.
CUPPA: high, squeaky, breathless cartoon voice with fast panicked delivery that cracks on the high notes.
BIG CAN: loud, raspy, over-caffeinated gym-bro voice with rapid-fire shouted delivery and a jittery stutter.
Characters speak ONLY the exact quoted lines below, in order. No extra dialogue, ad-libs or background voices.

TIMELINE

[0:00–0:02.5] SHOT 1 (HOOK). Low-angle close-up at the top of a steel ladder bolted to a column 40 storeys up. CUPPA clings to the top rung with one tiny arm, legs kicking wildly over the dizzying drop to the clay city far below, coffee sloshing out of its lid in fat clay droplets. It looks straight up past the camera, eyes huge, and screams with precise lip-sync:
CUPPA: "WAIT FOR MEEEE!"
Camera: fast push-in toward its face. Sound: whistling high-altitude wind, a creaking ladder, sloshing liquid. Music: none yet.

[0:02.5–0:05] SHOT 2. Medium shot of DALE sitting calmly on a steel I-beam, harness lanyard clipped, a dented lunchbox beside him, legs dangling over the city at sunrise. He glances down over his shoulder toward the ladder, unimpressed, then looks at the camera and speaks with precise lip-sync:
DALE: "Coffee can't climb, bud."
Camera: static, slight handheld sway. Sound: wind; a tiny faraway "noooo" from below as he speaks, kept under his voice. Music: a jaunty job-site groove kicks in on his last word, with a beat built from construction sounds (hammer taps as the snare, ratchet clicks as hi-hats, a rivet-gun stutter fill) and a whistled melody on top.

[0:05–0:07.5] SHOT 3. BIG CAN hauls itself up onto the far end of the same beam, vibrating so hard the steel rattles, sweating sugary clay crystals, eyes twitching. It flexes its veiny little arms toward camera and shouts with precise lip-sync:
BIG CAN: "THREE HUNDRED MILLIGRAMS, BABY! LET'S GOOO!"
DALE stays silent and does not lip-sync. Camera: quick whip-pan from Dale to Big Can. Sound: fizzing carbonation, rattling steel, an electric buzz. Music: the groove continues, ducked under the shout.

[0:07.5–0:10.5] SHOT 4. DALE turns to BIG CAN, deadpan, and speaks with precise lip-sync:
DALE: "I'm framing a floor, not launching a rocket."
As he finishes, BIG CAN shakes violently, its pull tab pops, and it blasts straight UP into the sunrise sky like a fizzing rocket, trailing a spiral of foam, until it's a tiny twinkle. Nothing falls downward. Big Can stays silent during Dale's line. Camera: tilt up following the can. Sound: a tab pop, a rocket whoosh, a distant "boing" twinkle. Music: one comic brass stab on the launch.

[0:10.5–0:13.5] SHOT 5. Close-up at chest height. DALE unzips his vest chest pocket with his gloved hand and pulls out the photorealistic Cool Mint tin (@image1). He flicks the lid open with his thumb in one smooth, satisfying motion, revealing the ring of pouches, then looks at camera and speaks with precise lip-sync:
DALE: "This rides up with me. Nicotine free. Eighty milligrams."
Camera: slow push-in onto the tin; the lettering stays sharp and readable. Sound: a zipper; then the signature crisp metallic tin-lid CLICK landing exactly on a beat. Music: groove continues, ducked under the dialogue.

[0:13.5–0:16] SHOT 6. Wider shot. The closed tin is back in DALE's vest pocket. He grips a tethered impact wrench with BOTH gloved hands and drives a bolt into a beam connection; clay sparks fly and he gives a satisfied nod. His mouth is not visible and he does not lip-sync; his voice is heard off-screen:
DALE (off-screen): "No cup. No can. Both hands on the job."
Camera: low angle, slow arc around him. Sound: the rattling brrrt of the impact wrench, synced to the music as a drum fill. Never show a pouch going into a mouth.

[0:16–0:19] SHOT 7. Macro close-up along the top flange of a steel beam. The three photorealistic tins sit in a row like giant rivets: Cool Mint (@image1), Watermelon Ice (@image2), Mixed Berry (@image3), left to right. Their lids pop open one after another on the beat, each releasing a burst of clay particles: frosty mint snowflakes, a juicy watermelon splash with seeds, a swirl of berry confetti. [0:19–0:20.5] SHOT 8. Back on the beam. CUPPA finally flops over the edge onto the steel, exhausted and dishevelled, lid askew, no steam left, a tiny icicle hanging from its sip hole. It looks up at camera, wheezing, and speaks with precise lip-sync:
CUPPA: "...I'm cold now."
DALE, beside it, stays silent and gives one slow, sympathetic pat on its lid. Sound: a wheeze; a tiny icy tinkle. Music: drops to a single held note for the joke.

[0:20.5–0:23] SHOT 9 (END). Hero shot: the photorealistic Cool Mint tin (@image1) stands centred on a steel beam flange, the sunrise flaring warmly behind it, the tower crane and clay city softly blurred in the background, DALE's gloved hand resting just beside it. Hold this frame to the end. DALE's voice is heard off-screen, with no one on camera speaking:

Camera: slow push-in, settled. Sound: the signature tin-lid CLICK on the downbeat, then the music resolves on a final hammer-tap hit.

AUDIO MIX
Dialogue is always the loudest and clearest element. Duck the music about 10 dB under every line. The music is an original, lyric-free, upbeat whistled work-song groove whose percussion is made of construction sounds (hammer taps, ratchet clicks, rivet-gun fills). The photoreal tin-lid CLICK is the recurring brand sound, always crisp and always on a beat. Effects are playful and short, and never cover a spoken word.

CONSTRAINTS

Nothing falls from height; the can rockets upward. Characters keep their exact designs, colours and proportions in every shot. The tins keep their exact shape, finish and rim colours and never melt, stretch, duplicate or morph. Speak only the quoted lines, word for word.

## If something goes wrong

- **Tin looks fake or warped:** tin photo references fix most of it. If it's still off, generate shots 5, 7 and 9 separately with the tin photo as the first frame.
- **Characters drift between shots:** make a character sheet for Dale, Cuppa and Big Can first (one image each), add them as references, and regenerate.
- **Voice drift:** record or TTS the lines using the Voices block, upload the audio as a reference, and tell the model to use it as the dialogue track.
- **Prompt too long for the platform:** split it into two generations, 0–10.5 s (the gag) and 10.5–23 s (the product), repeating FORMAT, STYLE, CONTINUITY LOCK, VOICES, AUDIO MIX and CONSTRAINTS in both.
- **Before you spend:** show the cut to two or three real tradespeople. If they laugh at shot 4 and can say the tagline back, you're good.
