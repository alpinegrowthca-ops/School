## Product facts [VERIFIED in dossier]

- Nicotine-free caffeine pouch. Per pouch: **80 mg caffeine, 80 mg L-theanine, 40 mg L-tyrosine, vitamin B6, vitamin B12.**
- **15 pouches per tin.** This matches the US nicotine-pouch can standard (PMI 8-K, §4.1).
- Flavors: **Cool Mint, Watermelon Ice, Mixed Berry.** Same 80 mg in all three (§10.C).
- DTC Shopify, premium positioning. Price TBD. The dossier recommends $12–15 one-time and $8–10 on subscription (§5.4). **Never put a price, discount, or guarantee in a script or storyboard until the brand confirms it.**
- 0 g sugar and 0 calories, per the dossier's comparison table (§10.D). Confirm this against the final label.

## Positioning priorities (ranked by the dossier's conversion evidence)

1. **“Nicotine free” in the first three words** of every ad, on the tin face, and in the title (§4.5). Buyers assume a pouch contains nicotine, and Walmart shelves the category under Smoking Cessation (§0.2 C).
2. **Same 15-pouch tin format as the nicotine user already carries.** This is the crossover beachhead (§4.1, §4.6). Show the format; never claim cessation (see guardrails).
3. **Disclosure is the villain's opposite.** The villain is *not knowing*: proprietary blends and doses kept off the label. The villain is never caffeine and never energy drinks as such (§7.5, §10.F).
4. **Dose anchor:** 80 mg is about the caffeine in a standard 8.4 oz energy drink (§2.1). The dossier suggests naming the brand (“one pouch, one Red Bull”). Get legal sign-off before naming any competitor brand. Default to the generic wording.
5. **Flavor as a primary axis.** Flavor comes up in 45% of review mentions and energy in 1% (§3.1). DESIRE has three flavors against Roon's one.
6. **Highest disclosed L-theanine per mg of caffeine** of any pouch brand that publishes doses (80:80 vs Roon's 80:60, §5.2).
7. **Format lineage:** the US Army spent six years at Walter Reed developing non-drink caffeine (Stay Alert gum, §8.1). This supports the format, not DESIRE. Don't borrow its absorption numbers, and never imply military endorsement.
8. **Honest expectation-setting:** it is 80 mg, not a 300 mg pre-workout, and says so (§10.C).

Saturated angles to avoid as lead messages: “no crash, no jitters”, “zero nicotine / zero sugar” as the whole pitch, milligram maximalism, ingredient-stack arms races, “best of 2026” lists (§6.3).

## Claim guardrails

Allowed, if worded exactly this carefully:

- ✅ “Nicotine free.” ✅ “The same tin / same format, without the nicotine.” ✅ Attributed customer quotes in customers' own words (⚠ still needs legal review, §9.5).
- ✅ Jagim et al., *Nutrients* 2019: the average top-100 pre-workout had about 18 ingredients, about 8 of them inside proprietary blends with undisclosed amounts (§7.2).
- ✅ Some mainstream energy drinks carry 300 mg per can. EFSA considers single doses up to 200 mg safe for healthy adults (§2.3, §7.3).
- ✅ Caffeine with L-theanine is one of the most studied pairings in cognitive-supplement research (§8.2). DESIRE's 1:1 ratio is **below** the studied range (1:1.25–1:2.4). Never say DESIRE matches or replicates the research.

Never write, show, or imply:

- ❌ Quitting, stopping, reducing, or replacing nicotine or tobacco: “quit Zyn”, “cessation aid”, “nicotine replacement”, “helps you stop”. This can turn the product into a regulated drug or medicine (§9.5). Don't show a nicotine tin being thrown away or swapped for DESIRE.
- ❌ Any claim about L-tyrosine. 40 mg is far below studied doses. List it only as an ingredient (§8.3).
- ❌ That caffeine itself is dangerous, or that energy drinks cause cardiac events (§7.4–7.5).
- ❌ Panera, Monster, Bang, Celsius, Red Bull, Zyn, Roon or other competitor names, logos, or recognizable trade dress, unless legal approves (§7.5).
- ❌ Real victims' names or likenesses (Sarah Katz and others). The dossier itself says to handle this with care.
- ❌ Flavor duration, onset time, or how long an effect lasts. None of these has been measured (§10.H items 6 and 10).
- ❌ The duration fight with Roon (6–8 h). DESIRE's counter is precision, not length (§5.2).
- ❌ Alertness, focus, or concentration claims in **EU/UK** creative. No authorized caffeine health claim exists there (§9.2–9.3). US structure/function wording needs the standard DSHEA disclaimer (§9.4).
- ❌ B12 “in milligrams”. B12 is usually dosed in micrograms. Say “by amount”.

## Visual and casting guardrails

- Adults only. Recurring humans should read as 25+. Nothing school-, campus-, or teen-coded (§9.5). End every ad with an 18+ line.
- Keep Watermelon Ice and Mixed Berry away from candy-store or cartoon-fruit imagery that reads as youth-directed (§9.5).
- Personify the villain as an **unlabeled supplement tub** (the undisclosed blend) and an **oversized generic energy can** (a dose you can't see). Give them no real logos or trade dress.
- Show the product as a clean, non-speaking hero object. The brand voice works best as an off-screen narrator: calm, precise, no hype.
- Military lineage: show objects only (a generic ration box, a plain gum pack), with no insignia and no soldiers holding DESIRE.

## Market label requirements

- **US (default):** likely a dietary supplement. Supplement Facts must state caffeine per serving. Add the structure/function disclaimer to any benefit claim (§9.4).
- **Canada:** above 56 mg per serving, the label needs “High caffeine content” and “Do not eat on the same day as any other source of caffeine”. An 80 mg pouch triggers both. Whether an oral pouch fits a permitted supplemented-food category at all is unresolved (§9.1). Add these to the end card for any Canadian cut.
- **EU/UK:** no caffeine health claims. Energy messaging can only ride on authorized B6/B12 claims if the doses meet the significant-amount threshold (§9.2–9.3).
