# Subject Playbooks

Adjust the core note and exam format to the discipline. When a course mixes several of these, combine the relevant playbooks.

## Math, physics, engineering, quantitative economics

- **Notes**: definitions → theorems or laws (with conditions) → derivations (key steps plus the *idea* behind them) → **worked examples** → "pattern recognition" cues (*when you see…, use…*).
- **Every formula**: a variable table with units, assumptions and validity range, plus a dimensional check.
- **Worked examples are the core.** Include 2–3 per method. Then use **faded examples** in quizzes: first fully worked, then with steps blanked for the student to fill, then an independent problem.
- **Problem-type catalogue** in the study guide: a list of every problem type seen in lectures and problem sets, with the recognition cue and the first step. Interleaved practice trains picking the right method.
- **Practice exams**: mostly multi-part problems. Include "set up but don't solve" items (which test strategy selection) and conceptual "explain why the answer is reasonable" items.
- **Common errors** to use as distractors: sign conventions, unit conversions, forgetting a term, applying a formula outside its assumptions, algebra slips.

## Chemistry

- Mechanisms as numbered steps with electron-flow descriptions ("lone pair on O attacks C=O carbon…"). Reaction summary tables (Reagents | Conditions | Product | Mechanism type | Stereochem).
- Stoichiometry and equilibrium calculations as worked examples.
- Periodic-trend and "predict the product" questions for application.

## Biology, biochemistry, medicine, nursing, health sciences

- **Process tables**: Step | Location | Inputs → Outputs | Enzymes or regulators | Why it matters. Add a flowchart.
- **Structure → function** for every structure. "It is shaped this way because…"
- **Matrix notes** for classifications (cell types, drugs, pathogens, hormones): Name | Source | Target | Mechanism | Effect | Clinical note.
- **Regulation and "what if" questions**: knockouts, inhibitors, mutations, disease states ("If enzyme X is deficient, what accumulates?").
- **Clinical vignettes** (medicine and nursing): scenario-based MCQs in board-exam style, with a single best answer.
- **Mnemonics** are fine for arbitrary lists (cranial nerves), but pair each with understanding cards.
- Anatomy: describe images precisely. Label-the-diagram questions become "Which structure lies immediately posterior to X?"

## Computer science

- **Code snippets**: small, runnable, commented. Always state time and space complexity and the edge cases.
- **Trace tables** for algorithms (the state of variables and data structures after each step).
- **Comparison matrices** for data structures and algorithms (operation costs, stability, when to use).
- Exams: code-tracing ("what does this print?"), bug-finding, complexity analysis, "write a function that…", and design trade-offs.
- Theory courses: definitions → theorems → proof techniques. Practice with "prove or disprove".

## Humanities: history, literature, philosophy, religious studies

- **Notes**: thesis-driven. For each topic: *main argument or interpretation → evidence → significance → counter-views*.
- **Timelines** (Mermaid `timeline`) for history, and **who-argued-what matrices** for philosophy and theory (Thinker | Core claim | Key concept | Critique of | Critiqued by).
- **Key passages and quotations**: short excerpts with speaker or author, context, and why they matter. These are ideal for identification or significance questions.
- **Themes across texts**: a matrix of Text × Theme.
- **Practice exams**: IDs ("identify and give significance"), passage analysis, compare/contrast essays, and thesis-driven essay prompts **with outlines**. Rubric: thesis, evidence, analysis, counterargument, organization. Also include "Write a thesis statement for this prompt" drills (fast retrieval of argument structures).

## Social sciences: psychology, sociology, political science, economics

- Theories as **claim → mechanism → evidence (key studies: who, design, finding) → limitations**.
- A key studies table: Study | Researchers (year) | Method | Finding | Significance | Critique.
- Research methods: be able to identify the IV/DV, confounds, and validity threats in novel study descriptions.
- Economics: graphs are central. Describe every graph (axes, curves, what shifts them) and practice "shift the curve" questions.

## Law

- **Case briefs**: Facts | Issue | Rule | Holding | Reasoning | Significance (IRAC-aligned).
- **Rule statements** with elements broken out, plus exceptions.
- Practice with **fact-pattern (issue-spotting) hypotheticals**, answered in IRAC form with a model answer and a rubric of the issues to spot.
- Outline-style study guides organized by doctrine, not by lecture.

## Languages

- Vocabulary goes into flashcards (both directions, with example sentences and gender or conjugation info).
- Grammar notes: the rule → form table → examples → exceptions → common errors.
- Practice: translation both ways, fill-in with the correct form, error correction, short production (write 3 sentences using…).
- Spacing matters most here. Recommend daily Anki reviews.

## Business, accounting, finance

- Frameworks with a worked case application. Formulas (ratios, NPV) with worked examples and interpretation ("a current ratio of 0.8 means…").
- Case-based exam questions: give a mini-case and ask for analysis and a recommendation, with a rubric.

## Lab courses

- Pre-lab notes: purpose, principle, procedure flow, safety, expected results, and the calculations.
- Post-lab: sources of error, and interpretation of the data. Practice questions on experimental design and data interpretation.
